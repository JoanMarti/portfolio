## The problem

By 2019 streaming was mainstream and recommendations were mature, but they all worked the same way: *because you listened to X*. Younger listeners, shaped by TikTok and Instagram, discovered music by mood and moment. They wanted novelty without configuring filters.

> Young listeners want a fast, effortless way to discover music that matches how they feel right now — without getting trapped in the same algorithmic loop.

## What I did

As Product Design Lead I owned product definition and UX: the discovery plan, recruiting for interviews and field tests, product hypotheses, MVP scope, success metrics, workshops and the story for investors.

**Discovery.** Hotjar and Google Analytics to find high-interest entry points, a branching Typeform survey to segment motivations, and in-depth interviews about frustration with repetition and how people look for “something new”. The insight: the problem wasn’t a lack of recommendations. It was a lack of **novelty** and of **relevance to the moment**.

**Ideation.** How-might-we questions, Crazy 8s and impact/effort mapping led to the key idea: an optional selfie as a playful mood check that produces a recommendation set in seconds.

## Key decisions

**Test two entry flows against each other.** Flow A used the selfie; Flow B used quick mood chips and sliders. We tested speed, clarity, fun versus “creepiness”, consent expectations and willingness to connect a streaming account.

**Be a layer, not a catalogue.** Instead of licensing music, users connected Spotify or Apple Music and played tracks there. That kept the MVP focused on what was new — discovery — and on the engagement loop.

**Design responsible AI from the start.** Because the concept touches emotion inference from a face, safeguards were part of the design, not a legal afterthought:
- Selfie entry is opt-in; manual mood selection is always available.
- Clear microcopy explains what the camera is used for.
- No images stored by default; on-device inference preferred.
- Easy to skip, revoke and delete.
- Mood treated as a soft signal, never as a diagnosis.

## Results

Field tests with 50+ participants across different times of day. Flow A was consistently seen as more fun, more novel and quicker to decide with. The MVP was then measured on discovery completion, saves and shares, and short-term return visits, to support fundraising conversations.

## What I’d do next

Combine mood with context (time, activity) and explicit preferences; add a “more new / more familiar” control; A/B test the consent step; and measure long-term retention by cohort.
